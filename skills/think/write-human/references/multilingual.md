# Non-English text

The structural tells of AI (uniformity, false ranges, conversational padding, emotional flatline) cross language boundaries. Apply every rule in `ai-patterns.md` to any language, plus the adaptations below.

## Translated AI-isms

LLMs lean on English training data and often translate English AI-isms literally into the target language. Look for direct translations of the Tier 1 and Tier 2 words in `vocabulary.md`.

- German: "delve" becomes "eintauchen", "seamless" becomes "nahtlos", "unleash" becomes "entfesseln".
- Spanish: "In today's fast-paced world" becomes "En el mundo acelerado de hoy", "delve" becomes "adentrarse".

Treat literal translations of the English tells as Tier 1 offenses in the target language. Replace them with phrasing a native writer would use.

## Register and pronoun dissonance

Many languages split formal and informal address (*Sie*/*du*, *vous*/*tu*, *usted*/*tú*). AI text slips between the two, or pairs a stiff formal register with gushing adjectives (bureaucratic grammar describing a "magical, game-changing journey").

Pick one register from the context, hold it for the whole piece, and keep the emotional temperature in line with that level of formality.

## Transition boilerplate

AI generates the same scaffolding in every language. Strip the equivalents of "In conclusion" (*En conclusión*, *Zusammenfassend lässt sich sagen*, *En résumé*), "Let's explore" (*Vamos a explorar*, *Lassen Sie uns untersuchen*, *Découvrons*) and "It is important to note".

## German

Keep the core rules and watch for literal translations from English, an overly formal tone, and mixed registers.

1. Decide Sie or du before writing. In product and brand copy it signals the relationship, so make it a conscious choice and keep it everywhere.
2. No bold-plus-colon lead-ins ("**Die Lösung:**"). Write normal sentences.
3. No empty balance phrases ("Einerseits ... andererseits ..."). Be specific about what weighs on each side.
4. Cut bureaucratic buzzwords and filler transitions ("im Rahmen von", "hinsichtlich", "es sei darauf hingewiesen, dass").
5. Cut both kinds of padding: American enthusiasm ("großartig", "spannend") and German qualification ("grundsätzlich", "gewissermaßen", "im Prinzip"). Both are noise.
