# Transitions

Use a transition only when the connection between two sentences isn't already obvious. If removing it doesn't break the flow, remove it. The best paragraph link is usually content: the first sentence of the new paragraph picks up a noun or claim from the last one.

This list is pruned against `ai-patterns.md` and `vocabulary.md`. Anything those files flag ("Moreover", "That said", "When it comes to", "Let's look at", "Here's the key takeaway", "Research shows" without a source, "In conclusion") is deliberately missing here.

Adapted from the University of Manchester Academic Phrasebank and the Plain English Campaign.

## Addition

Also · And · Plus · On top of that · Beyond that · There's also …

## Contrast

But · However · Yet · In contrast · Unlike X, Y … · Even so

Use "while X, Y" only when both halves are specific; vague versions are the false-concession tell.

## Similarity

Similarly · Likewise · The same applies to … · This mirrors …

## Cause and effect

So · This means … · As a result · That's why … · Because of this · This leads to …

## Examples

For example · For instance · Take X · Say you want to … · Like when …

## Referring back

Name the thing instead of pointing at the text: "The 30-day limit also explains …" beats "As mentioned earlier, …". Use "Going back to X" only after a real detour.

## Evidence

Always name the source.

- According to {named source}, …
- {Named source} found that …
- Data from {named source} shows …
- As {named expert} puts it, …

## Hedges

A hedge must say where the doubt sits: "in most cases", "for teams under 50 people", "in the average case", "largely". Hedges that name no scope ("it appears that", "may potentially") get cut. `essay.md` has the same rule.

## Steps and lists

Introduce a list with what it is, not with a frame: "Three settings control this:" or "To connect a sensor:" rather than "Here's what you need to know:".

## Guidelines

1. Match tone to the audience. B2B can be slightly more formal; B2C can be conversational.
2. Don't repeat the same transition within a few paragraphs, but don't cycle synonyms to avoid it either.
3. Don't signpost every sentence. Trust the reader.
4. Front-load the key word: "Cost is the second problem" instead of "Another thing to consider is cost".
5. Read it aloud. If it sounds forced, cut it.
