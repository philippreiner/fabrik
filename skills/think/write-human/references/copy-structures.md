# Copy structures

Headline formulas, CTA patterns, section types and page templates. A toolkit, not a checklist: pick what fits, ignore the rest. Every formula is a slot-fill template, so fill the slots with specifics (a number, a named pain, the reader's own words). A formula filled with generic nouns is exactly the template-phrase tell from `vocabulary.md`.

## Headlines

The headline carries one message and must work without the subheadline. The subheadline adds specificity in one or two sentences and never repeats the headline in other words.

| Formula | Example | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| {Outcome} without {pain point} | Build your website without writing code | The pain is well known and the relief is clear |
| {Outcome} by {how the product makes it possible} | Generate more leads by seeing which companies visit your site | The mechanism is the differentiator |
| Turn {input} into {outcome} | Turn your sales into repeat customers | There's a clear before and after |
| {Outcome} in {timeframe} | Get your tax refund in 10 days | Speed is the point and you can prove the number |
| Never {unpleasant event} again | Never miss a sales opportunity again | You eliminate a recurring frustration |
| {Question naming the pain} | Hate returning stuff to Amazon? | The question is in the reader's own words (not a rhetorical stall) |
| Stop {pain}. Start {gain}. | Stop chasing invoices. Start getting paid on time. | Pain and gain mirror each other |
| {Product type} for {audience} | Advanced analytics for Shopify stores | Positioning against a known category |
| {Product type} for {audience} to {job} | An online whiteboard for teams to plan together | The job needs naming too |
| You don't have to {skill} to {outcome} | You don't have to be an SEO pro to rank higher | The barrier is expertise |
| The {category} that {differentiator} | The CRM that updates itself | One differentiator is strong enough to carry the line |
| {Number} {people} use {product} to {outcome} | 50,000 marketers use Drip to send better emails | You have a real, current number |
| {Key benefit} | Sound clear in online meetings | The benefit is self-explanatory |
| Get {benefit} from your {thing} | Get more revenue from your existing traffic | The reader already owns the input |
| Everything you need to {outcome} | Everything you need to launch your course | The product genuinely covers the full job |

Use "Finally, …" and "What if you could …?" sparingly; both read as hype to European audiences.

## CTAs

A CTA says what happens next. The reader should know exactly what clicking does.

Formula: {action verb} + {what they get} + {qualifier if needed}.

Strong: "Start free trial", "See {product} in action", "Create your first {thing}", "Book a demo", "Download the guide".

Weak: "Submit" (says nothing), "Sign up" (for what?), "Learn more" (vague), "Click here" (meaningless), "Get started" (with what?).

One primary CTA per view. A secondary CTA offers a lower commitment ("See how it works" next to "Start free trial").

## Section types

Not every page needs all of them. Order them as an argument down the page.

- **Hero.** One value proposition, one CTA, a supporting visual. Within five seconds the visitor knows what this is, who it's for and what to do next.
- **Social proof.** Logos, a key metric, a short testimonial. Specific beats impressive: a quote from a named person at a named company outweighs "trusted by thousands". Recognizable logos beat many logos.
- **Problem.** Show you understand the reader's situation in their language: the frustration, the workaround, the cost of the status quo. Open with their concrete situation, not a stock opener like "We've all been there".
- **Solution or benefits.** Feature, then benefit, then outcome. Three to five points; more dilutes the message. Each gets a headline (the outcome), one or two sentences on how, and proof if you have it.
- **How it works.** Three or four numbered steps, each a simple verb plus what the reader gets ("Connect your tools (takes 2 minutes)").
- **Objection handling.** FAQ, comparisons (against competitors or the status quo, such as spreadsheets), guarantees. Address real hesitations from customer language in the vault, not imagined ones.
- **Testimonials and case studies.** Full name, role, company. Specific results ("increased conversions by 32%") and before/after context. Drop testimonials that only say "Great product!" or "Easy to use!".
- **Use cases and personas.** Help visitors self-identify ("For maintenance planners who need X").
- **Integrations.** Logos of tools you connect with.
- **Founder story.** Why you built this and what you believe.
- **Demo or tour.** Video, interactive demo, GIF previews.
- **Pricing preview.** A starting price moves decision-makers forward.
- **Risk reversal.** Free trial terms, no credit card, cancel anytime.
- **Final CTA.** Restate the value in one line, repeat the primary CTA, add risk reversal.

## Page types

- **Homepage.** Serves several audiences without being generic. Leads with the broadest value proposition and gives each intent its own path.
- **Landing page.** One message, one CTA, headline matched exactly to the traffic source. The full argument sits on one page.
- **Pricing page.** Helps visitors choose, not just compare. Makes the recommended plan obvious. Plain plan names; cute names confuse.
- **Feature page.** Feature, benefit, outcome, then use cases and a clear path to try or buy.
- **About page.** Why the company exists, tied to what customers get. Still ends in a CTA; no page is a dead end.

## Page templates

A page that runs Hero, Feature 1, Feature 2, Feature 3, Feature 4, CTA is a list, not an argument. Prefer one of these:

**Full landing page:** hero, social proof bar, problem, how it works, two or three benefits, testimonial, use cases, comparison, case study snippet, FAQ, final CTA with guarantee.

**Compact (ad traffic):** hero, social proof bar, three benefits, testimonial, how it works, final CTA with guarantee.

**Enterprise/B2B:** outcome-focused hero, logo bar, business problem, solution overview, use cases by role, security and compliance, integrations, case study with metrics, ROI section, demo CTA.

**Product launch:** launch announcement hero, demo video, three to five feature highlights, before/after, early testimonials, launch pricing or early access (with a real deadline only if one exists), CTA.
