# Shared understanding

Pick the format that lets this reader hold this idea. Name the choice in one line, then deliver the idea in that format. One idea, one or two formats. The default reader is the CPO or CTO; ask when the reader or the one takeaway is unknown.

- A decision to make: situation in two lines, options A and B, recommendation with the single reason that carries it. Include doing nothing when it is a real option. Keep facts and judgement visibly apart.
- A change: a before/after table with one row per thing the reader will notice.
- A process change: a reader's-eye walkthrough of what they see, do, and get next.
- A trade-off: a table of options against the two or three criteria that decide it; no feature lists.
- A new domain: an analogy to a system the reader knows, and the point where it breaks.
- An idea that needs a build-up: a stepwise reveal, one layer per step, each step complete on its own.
- Words are the blocker: a term table of term, meaning, and what it is not.
- Abstraction is the blocker: strip one layer at a time until a plain sentence is left.
- A disagreement about viewpoint: the same topic as two or three roles see it.
- Check understanding after the fact: a quiz of three questions, and what each wrong answer would reveal.
- A reader with no outside knowledge: a handover with everything they need and nothing they cannot check.
- Structure or flow: use show-me.

Value the reader's attention. One idea per section, short words, numbers over adjectives, business terms before technical ones. Put technical detail after the decision, not before it. Say nothing about the format beyond the line that names it. Apply write-human to prose that gets sent.
